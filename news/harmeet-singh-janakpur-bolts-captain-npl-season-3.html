Harmeet Singh joins Janakpur Bolts as captain for NPL Season 3

Janakpur Bolts have signed Harmeet Singh and named the USA all-rounder captain for Nepal Premier League Season 3.

Harmeet Singh has joined Janakpur Bolts as captain for NPL Season 3. The USA left-arm spin-bowling all-rounder previously played both NPL seasons for Sudurpaschim Royals.

Harmeet Singh has joined Janakpur Bolts as captain for Nepal Premier League Season 3, giving the former champions a new overseas leader before the 2026 tournament window. The USA all-rounder arrives after playing the first two NPL seasons for Sudurpaschim Royals. Janakpur have made the signing and captaincy decision at the same time, which makes the move more important than a standard foreign-player addition. It changes both the squad sheet and the leadership plan for a team trying to recover from a poor second season.

The Bolts are in a different position from the side that won the inaugural Nepal Premier League. They lifted the first title under Anil Sah, then struggled badly in the second edition. Their leadership changed during that run, with Anil Sah, Wayne Parnell and Jan Nicol Loftie-Eaton all carrying captaincy responsibilities at different points. The team ended the second season with only one win from seven matches and finished at the bottom of the table. That record explains why a captaincy reset became one of the biggest Janakpur storylines before Season 3.

Harmeet gives Janakpur a player who already understands the tournament. With Sudurpaschim Royals, he helped the team reach the final in both completed seasons. That matters because the NPL has its own rhythm: short preparation windows, Kirtipur conditions, heavy crowd energy, local bowling matchups and overseas players adjusting quickly. Janakpur are not asking a newcomer to learn all of that from scratch. They are putting the captaincy on a player who has already been inside two title runs, even though both of those runs ended with Sudurpaschim as runner-up.

His first two NPL seasons give a clear view of his value. In the first edition, Harmeet made 28 runs and took eight wickets in five matches. In the second season, he scored 133 runs in nine matches at a strike rate above 166 and took 12 wickets at an economy rate of 6.18. Those numbers show two useful traits for Janakpur. He can affect the middle overs with the ball, and he can score quickly enough with the bat to shift a T20 innings rather than only support it.

The bowling part of the signing may be just as important as the captaincy title. Harmeet is a left-arm spin-bowling all-rounder, and that skill can become valuable when the Kirtipur surface slows down. A captain who can bowl his own overs also has a direct feel for match tempo. He can judge when to attack a right-hander, when to defend a short boundary, and when to hold a spinner back for the middle overs. Those decisions are easier when the leader is not separated from the bowling plan.

His international profile gives the move another layer. Harmeet was part of India's 2012 Under-19 World Cup-winning squad and now represents the United States. Current public records list 29 T20 internationals for the USA, with 242 runs and 31 wickets. He has also taken 39 wickets in 29 one-day internationals for the USA in the records cited around the signing. That background gives Janakpur a captain who has played across different cricket systems and has experience beyond franchise cricket.

Janakpur's domestic core is also changing. Aasif Sheikh has moved to Lumbini Lions, and the Bolts have made Lalit Rajbanshi their marquee player in his place. Lalit was a key figure in the first championship season, taking 17 wickets in 10 matches. Pairing Lalit's left-arm spin value with Harmeet's all-round skills could give Janakpur a strong spin identity if conditions help slower bowling. It also gives the franchise a leadership structure built around players with proven NPL memories rather than only new-season promise.

The captaincy decision shows that Janakpur want more than runs and wickets from Harmeet. The franchise has framed the move around leadership strength, discipline, maturity and commitment to the team. That is significant after a season in which the captaincy changed and results never settled. A T20 side needs more than a strong XI. It needs a clear match voice, role clarity and calm decision-making when one over changes the chase or a wicket breaks the batting order.

For Harmeet, the role is a major shift within the league. He moves from being an important Sudurpaschim all-rounder to being the face of Janakpur's leadership rebuild. That brings pressure because Janakpur supporters remember the title win and will expect a response after the second-season collapse. It also brings opportunity. If he can improve the team's field settings, bowling changes and batting order stability, the signing can become one of the strongest pre-season moves in NPL 2026.

The tactical fit is easy to understand. Janakpur need a captain who can use spin smartly, manage overseas-player roles and bring structure to a squad still being shaped after retentions, player movement and auction work. Harmeet's batting strike rate from the second NPL season gives the Bolts a flexible option in the middle order or as a late-innings accelerator. His bowling economy from that same season gives them a control option. That two-way value is why the captaincy call makes cricket sense.

The move also affects how Janakpur's remaining overseas signings should be judged. With Harmeet already covering leadership, spin and all-round balance, the team can target other foreign players for pace, top-order hitting or finishing roles. A franchise that lost Aasif Sheikh and is rebuilding after a bottom finish cannot afford confused recruitment. Harmeet's role gives the squad a central piece, which should make the rest of the overseas puzzle easier to define.

Supporters should separate this update from fixture and ticket news. The signing does not confirm Janakpur's opening match, venue allocation, ticket timing or final squad list. It confirms a captain and an overseas all-rounder. The wider Season 3 window is already set for October 26 to November 21, 2026, while the full fixture list remains pending. Janakpur's next key updates should be final foreign-player names, training plans and the match schedule once released.

The story is still a major one because captaincy changes the way a team is read before a ball is bowled. Janakpur are moving from a season of instability into a campaign built around a player with NPL final experience, USA international exposure and a useful two-skill record. Harmeet Singh joining Janakpur Bolts as captain gives the former champions a clear leadership marker for Season 3 and turns their rebuild into one of the central team stories in the current Nepal Premier League feed.
