Ajantha Mendis named Pokhara Avengers head coach for NPL Season 3

Pokhara Avengers have appointed former Sri Lanka mystery spinner Ajantha Mendis as head coach before Nepal Premier League Season 3.

Jul 20, 2026 NPL 2026 Nepal Premier League
Reading5 min
Sources4
Words955+

Pokhara Avengers have appointed former Sri Lanka spinner Ajantha Mendis as head coach for Nepal Premier League Season 3, giving the franchise a specialist tactical voice before the 2026 campaign. The move brings one of modern cricket's best-known mystery spin figures into a team still trying to turn promise into a first NPL playoff appearance. For Pokhara supporters, the appointment is more than a coaching headline. It gives the squad a clear bowling identity before training plans, overseas-player decisions and final match combinations begin to settle.

Mendis joins a franchise that has retained Kushal Bhurtel as its most visible domestic figure and kept a local group that includes Sagar Dhakal, Arjun Kumal, Bipin Khatri, Aakash Chand, Kiran Thagunna and Dinesh Kharel. That retained base gives the new coach enough continuity to work with, while the Season 3 auction and later squad additions give him room to shape the side. Pokhara have finished seventh and fifth in the first two league seasons, so a coaching reset was always going to be watched closely.

The strongest part of the appointment is the match between Mendis' playing profile and Pokhara's cricket needs. He was one of Sri Lanka's most influential mystery spinners during the late 2000s, using carrom-ball variations, changes of pace and unusual release points to control batters across formats. His international record includes 19 Tests, 87 ODIs and 39 T20Is, with 288 wickets for Sri Lanka. A coach with that background can help a T20 team think more sharply about middle-over control and matchup bowling.

Pokhara already have spin and seam options who can gain from that kind of specialist coaching. Sagar Dhakal gives the side a left-arm spin option, while Aakash Chand and Bipin Khatri provide bowling variety around the domestic group. Mendis can work on field placement, pace changes, batting matchups and the mental side of defending small boundaries. In a short T20 league, those details can decide close games. A team does not need every bowler to be a mystery spinner to benefit from a coach who understands deception and pressure.

His playing career also brings big-match credibility. The 2008 Asia Cup final remains one of his defining performances, when he took 6 for 13 against India and helped Sri Lanka win the title. In Test cricket, his first series against India produced 26 wickets, one of the strongest starts by a spinner. In T20Is, his 6 for 8 against Zimbabwe in 2012 still stands among the best figures in men's T20I history. He was also part of Sri Lanka's 2014 ICC World T20 winning squad.

That history matters because Pokhara need more than routine coaching before Season 3. The Avengers have carried strong names in previous squads, yet the franchise has not converted those resources into a playoff run. A coach with international success can bring authority to selection meetings and training sessions, but the real test is whether he can simplify roles. Pokhara need clarity on who attacks in the powerplay, who controls overs seven to fifteen, who finishes innings and which bowlers handle pressure at the end.

The appointment also changes the way Pokhara's auction and retention work should be judged. If the team wants to play through bowling discipline, it needs enough batting stability to defend tactical plans. If the team wants to attack harder with Kushal Bhurtel and other top-order players, it needs bowlers who can protect totals. Mendis cannot solve every squad question alone, but his arrival gives the cricket department a clearer framework for evaluating players and preparing combinations.

For young Nepali cricketers, the move can be valuable if it becomes practical coaching rather than name value. A coach with Mendis' background can teach variation control, release discipline, batter reading and over-by-over planning. Young bowlers often have skills but lack a clear method for using them under pressure. A short conversation about when to bowl quicker, when to change angle, when to hide a variation and when to trust a stock ball can have a real effect during a tight NPL match.

The wider Nepal Premier League context is also important. Season 3 is moving from auction stories into team-building stories. Player retention, domestic auction buys and overseas-player tracking have dominated recent coverage, but coaching appointments show how franchises plan to turn squads into results. Stronger coaching standards can raise the quality of the league because domestic players spend more time in structured T20 environments and learn from people who have solved high-level cricket problems.

Pokhara's first target under Mendis should be consistency. The team does not need to win every tactical battle immediately, but it must become harder to predict and harder to dominate in the middle overs. Better bowling plans can reduce chase pressure for batters, and clearer batting roles can give bowlers more defendable totals. If the franchise can connect those parts, the Season 3 playoff chase becomes more realistic.

The appointment also gives supporters a fresh reason to follow Pokhara before the tournament starts. Coaching announcements do not carry the same instant excitement as a star player signing, but they often reveal how a franchise thinks. Pokhara are signaling that they want a sharper cricket brain around the squad, especially in bowling and tactical planning. That is a useful direction for a team looking to move beyond mid-table finishes.

The next Pokhara updates to watch are overseas-player confirmations, final squad balance and any preseason plans involving spin or bowling camps. Mendis will be judged by how the Avengers perform once NPL Season 3 begins, not by the announcement alone. Still, the appointment gives Pokhara one of the clearest coaching storylines of the current Nepal Premier League build-up and makes their Season 3 preparation more interesting than it looked after the auction alone.
