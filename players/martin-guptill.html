Martin Guptill Player Profile

Early Life and Background

Martin Guptill was born on September 30, 1986, in Auckland, New Zealand, and developed a passion for cricket from a young age. He studied at Kelston Primary and later Kelston Boys' High School, where he dedicated himself to improving his game. At age 13, he suffered a serious accident when a forklift ran over his left foot, resulting in the loss of three toes. Despite this setback, he demonstrated remarkable resilience, later moving to Avondale College for his final school year where he served as a prefect. His family has cricket connections, with his cousin Michael Guptill-Bunce playing for Auckland.

Domestic and Franchise Career

Guptill began his domestic career with Auckland in the 2005–06 season and quickly established himself as a reliable opening batsman. Over the years, he gained valuable experience in global T20 leagues, representing teams like Derbyshire, Sydney Thunder, Guyana Amazon Warriors, Mumbai Indians, Kings XI Punjab, Worcestershire, Barbados Tridents, and Sunrisers Hyderabad. Recently, he has continued his career in leagues such as the Lanka Premier League and Legends League Cricket, maintaining his competitive edge after retiring from international cricket.

International Career Highlights

Guptill began his international journey in 2009 and quickly became a cornerstone of New Zealand's white-ball teams. He made an immediate impact with an unbeaten 122 on his ODI debut against West Indies. In 198 ODIs, he scored 7346 runs at an average of 41.73, including 18 centuries. In T20Is, he played 122 matches and scored 3531 runs with two centuries, striking at over 135. His Test career comprised 47 matches with 2586 runs at 29.38. He played crucial roles in New Zealand's runs to the 2015 and 2019 World Cup finals.

NPL 2024 Performance

In the inaugural NPL season last year, Guptill played six matches for Biratnagar Kings and scored 139 runs in seven innings. His average was 23.17 with a strike rate close to 120. He scored the league's first fifty, marking a significant moment in the tournament's history. Despite the team's struggles, his innings provided highlights throughout the season.

Records and Achievements

  • First New Zealand player to score a double century in ODIs (237* vs West Indies in 2015 World Cup)
  • Highest individual score in a World Cup match (237*)
  • Second-highest individual score in ODI cricket history
  • First New Zealand player to carry the bat in an ODI
  • Second batsman in the world to reach 3000 T20I runs
  • Sir Richard Hadlee Medal winner in 2011–12
  • Scored back-to-back unbeaten hundreds against England in 2013

Recent Updates and Retirement

In January 2026, Guptill retired from international cricket after 13 years, with his last match played in October 2022. This decision allowed him to focus exclusively on T20 leagues worldwide. His arrival in Nepal for NPL 2026 generated significant excitement among fans who appreciate his continued dedication to the sport. His strong start in the first match demonstrates his readiness for a productive season. As the league progresses, fans anticipate more explosive innings from this seasoned campaigner.
